MoMa-NMPScene-Scale Neural Motion Planning
for Mobile Manipulators

Closed-loop whole-body motion planning from partial depth and LiDAR observations.

Abstract

Mobile manipulators operating in homes and other complex environments must coordinate their base and arm over long horizons, navigating around obstacles, through narrow passages, and between constrained workspaces. Neural motion planners can provide fast closed-loop planning from partial observations, but scaling them to room-sized environments is difficult because generating high-quality training trajectories is itself a challenging motion-planning problem. To address this, we introduce MoMa-DemoSmith, a demonstration-generation pipeline that separates the problem into global path discovery for large complex scenes and local motion refinement for collision avoidance. This key separation enables the generation of millions of challenging whole-body trajectories across our procedurally generated scenes. These demonstrations are used to train MoMa-NMP in two stages, first through behavior cloning (BC), followed by privileged on-policy distillation, producing a closed-loop policy capable of robust whole-body planning across complex, partially observed environments.

MoMa-NMP

Policy Architecture and Training

MoMa-NMP takes depth and LiDAR point clouds, the robot state, and an end-effector goal pose as input, and predicts five steps of coordinated base-and-arm motion. We first train it by behavior cloning on MoMa-DemoSmith demonstrations. During on-policy fine-tuning, Geometric Fabrics uses ground-truth scene geometry to correct the frozen behavior-cloned policy’s actions and supervise the student. At deployment, the learned policy replans from onboard observations without access to ground-truth scene geometry.

Rendered Point-Cloud Observations

We reproduce partial observability in simulation with GPU-based sensor rendering. Points sampled from scene and robot geometry are projected through depth-camera and LiDAR renderers, which retain only the nearest return in each image pixel or angular bin. Occluded geometry is removed before the policy observes the scene.

Depth-camera and LiDAR points are combined with a directly sampled subset of the robot mesh, since robot geometry and joint state are known at deployment. The three point sets form a single PointNet++ input, with source features identifying each point type.

Ablation Studies+

Action-Target Annotation

Fabrics annotations improve behavior-cloning success from 47.0% to 64.8% compared with raw cuRobo actions.

On-Policy Distillation

Compared with the policy trained with behavior cloning (BC) only, privileged on-policy distillation improves simulation success from 64.8% to 87.0%. On compartment-to-compartment tasks, success increases from 56.6% with BC only to 84.4% after distillation.

Delta-Pose Inputs

Removing the base-frame and end-effector-frame delta-pose inputs reduces behavior-cloning success from 64.8% to 27.5% and increases collision-before-success from 34.1% to 56.9%.

Training Observation Model

Compared with training on complete scene point clouds, training on rendered depth and LiDAR improves real-world success from 54.5% to 79.5% on a separate set of 44 matched trials per policy. Both policies receive the same real depth-camera and LiDAR observations at deployment.

Limitations

Like many neural methods, MoMa-NMP struggles in scenes that diverge significantly from the training distribution. For example, it often collides with chairs, which are not well represented by either the pillars or tables in our training set.

Although MoMa-NMP demonstrates an ability to plan around partially occluded obstacles, it often collides with nearby small obstacles that are fully occluded from both the LiDAR and depth cameras.
